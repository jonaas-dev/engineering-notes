# Leadership

Notes on the parts of the job that decide whether a team ships: the economics of internal
quality, how feedback actually lands, who you hire, and what agile turned into on the way here.

## Notes

- [Is High Quality Software Worth the Cost?](is-high-quality-software-worth-the-cost.md) —
  the argument that internal quality is not traded against speed but is the mechanism of it,
  with the pay-off period measured in weeks.
- [Software engineering practices for development teams](software-engineering-practices.md) —
  concrete team practices: docs in the same repo as the code, generated test data instead of
  production dumps, and schema migrations boring enough to be routine.
- [How to Give Constructive Feedback That Actually Empowers Others](how-to-give-constructive-feedback.md) —
  behaviour over personality, and the four properties feedback needs to be usable:
  specificity, timeliness, relevance, empathy.
- [Startup Engineering Hiring Anti-Patterns](startup-engineering-hiring-anti-patterns.md) —
  the traps: hiring for a toolchain, hiring specialists too early, and getting comfortable
  with false negatives.
- [Doerr's Law on Product Teams](doerrs-law-on-product-teams.md) — missionaries over
  mercenaries, and the part that matters: a missionary is someone empowered to say no.
- [Agile at 20: The Failed Rebellion](agile-at-20-the-failed-rebellion.md) — how a manifesto
  about individuals and interactions ended up sold as processes and tools.
- [Employed in Tech for Years but Almost Never Worked](employed-in-tech-for-years-but-almost-never-worked.md)
  `[ES/CA]` — task bloating, ceremony mistaken for progress, and hype-driven adoption.
